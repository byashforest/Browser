A little more about Affect

I'm Ash Forest, a mover and transdisciplinary artist interested in the relationships between bodies, ecology, time, and perception.

I've always been fascinated by the nuances of movement. How things move, and how moving feels. The way pressure gently builds, swells, and explodes, the feelings of momentum meeting friction, the difference between fighting gravity and fully yielding into it.

Every movement is a negotiation of forces. And yet, movement is fleeting.

Video can preserve what a movement looked like, but we're still looking at a two-dimensional image of the body's outer surface, and the place it's in. We see what someone does, but not necessarily how it feels.

I've explored drawing movement in my journals, too, but there's still the challenge of translating something dynamic in space and time onto a flat surface.

Still, there's something about the intimacy of a journal that I love. Your words, your doodles, the marks you've made, without needing an image of you making them. I've wondered what it would mean to bring that same intimacy to movement.

What if you could sketch through how you move? Make marks with the qualities of movement itself? What if you could return to those gestures weeks or years later, and experience something of them again?

How it Works

Your phone contains tiny sensors that detect changes in motion. Affect uses acceleration data to translate those changes into a three-dimensional sketch. Hold your phone and capture a moment of movement. You might let a single gesture unfold, repeat a smaller movement, or respond to a piece of music. Once captured, you can turn your sketch, explore it from different perspectives, and watch it unfold again. Try repeating a gesture, changing its rhythm or intensity. See what changes, what remains, and what surprises you.
